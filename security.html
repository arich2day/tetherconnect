Security architecture

One new auditable
control. No MITM CA.
No data-residency exposure.

Cryptographic policy distribution, per-tenant isolation in your cloud account, per-request attestation, fail-closed-on-judge-timeout, three-tier judge authority. The honest threat model — in and out of scope — at the bottom.

Design principles

Four principles
shaping every decision.

Principle 01

Fail closed

When the agent cannot verify a current policy — network loss, signature mismatch, judge timeout in sync mode — it defaults to blocking. An agent that fails open is not a control.

Principle 02

No trust without verification

Policy is applied only after Ed25519 signature verification with a per-tenant public key pinned on first use. A policy that fails verification is discarded — the agent keeps enforcing the last verified policy or blocks egress entirely.

Principle 03

Payload never leaves for analysis

The deterministic floor and (when enabled) the judge run against the prompt locally. If you configure a cloud judge, calls go to the model vendor you chose — never a Tether-hosted service. A local judge keeps every decision on-device.

Principle 04

Defense in depth

A deterministic floor, an opt-in synchronous check, an advisory async review, signed policy distribution, and an audited console — independent layers, each capable of catching what the others miss. Advisory findings can be promoted into the floor, turning evidence into future enforcement.

No MITM root CA on the developer machine

The proxy does not
intercept TLS.

The Connect proxy is loopback-only and does not perform TLS interception. CONNECT tunnels are byte-copied bidirectionally. The proxy classifies the destination host on the CONNECT path and reads the request body only when an agent is pointed at the configured-upstream HTTP route (Tier B in the coverage taxonomy).

No root certificate is installed on the developer's machine. The same CA that decrypts a Cursor call decrypts the developer's banking session — Tether does not put either of those into play.

Workspace-tier TLS termination is reserved for the future managed VSCodium build — an opt-in CA install on a managed device, not a sneak-install on every developer's laptop.

Why this matters to you

You get the control without the thing developers and privacy teams fight hardest: a corporate certificate that can decrypt anything on the machine. Tether doesn't generate one, inject one, or read TLS-tunneled content.

It's a claim you can check yourself — there is no Tether root certificate to find in the trust store on any laptop. The source comment and the host-classification path are in the technical brief →

Three-tier judge authority

Who can block what,
when, and how to tell.

In one sentence: Tier 0 always blocks; Tier 1 is configurable — sync-block opt-in at deploy time, fail-closed on timeout when enabled; Tier 2 records but cannot block.

The full breakdown — what each tier can block, when, and how a decision is labelled so you never confuse an advisory signal with a guarantee — lives in the technical brief · judge authority →, with the code citations behind every line.

Per-request attestation

X-Tether-Policy-Version
on every response.

Ed25519 policy signing proves which bundle Overwatch signed and distributed. Per-request attestation closes the gap from which bundle exists to which bundle decided this specific request.

The proxy sets X-Tether-Policy-Version: <version> on every blocked 403 and every forwarded response. Every AI_PROXY_REQUEST event sent to Overwatch carries policyVersion. Every judge review carries it too.

An auditor with the event ID makes one call. GET /api/v1/attestation/<eventId> returns a single artifact: the decision, the signed policy bundle that made it, and the tenant's Ed25519 public key.

A standalone verifier — tools/verify-attestation.js, zero dependencies — re-checks that artifact offline: the Ed25519 signature, the binding of the decision to its policy version, and the exact controls in force. The signed bundle pins the judge model, so the attestation also covers which model decided. An auditor re-verifies a decision offline, cryptographically.

The chain — from the policy version on a request, to the signed bundle, to an offline signature check, to the controls in force — is the reproducible enforcement attestation. See the technical brief →

The point for your auditor

The evidence stands on its own. Your auditor pulls the signed bundle for the version that decided a request and verifies the signature offline — a standard cryptographic check they run with their own tools. If it verifies, the controls in the bundle are provably the ones in force at decision time.

No re-running Tether. No taking our word for it. The exact response shape and the verification command are in the technical brief →

Honest caveats. In-memory events do not survive Overwatch restart unless your SIEM adapter forwards them. The persistent audit trail is policy-audit.jsonl for policy publish actions — per-request event durability depends on SIEM forwarding. Tamper-evident per-version bundle retention (append-only WORM) is a documented gap for deployments requiring full forensic retention.

Per-tenant isolation · data residency

Your cloud account.
Your secrets. Your buckets.

Overwatch is provisioned in the customer's cloud account. One Cloud Run service (GCP) or ECS service (AWS) per tenant. Per-tenant GCS bucket / EFS file system for state. Per-tenant Secret Manager / Secrets Manager for tokens and the policy signing key.

No traffic flows through Tether-owned servers. The custom domain terminates on your Cloud Run instance. For EU and regulated buyers, customer data stays in your eu-west-1 or europe-west1 region by virtue of where you deploy.

The honest qualification. If you configure a cloud judge with OpenAI or another vendor, prompt previews flow to that vendor. This is your choice — the architecture supports a fully on-prem judge (a local model) that keeps all data within your environment.

Air-gap is architecturally coherent: a local proxy, a local judge, an on-prem control plane, and the syslog path — every component supports running with no internet dependency. The deploy detail is in the technical brief →

Threat model

What Tether covers.
And what it does not.

Honest security posture requires a clear threat model. Tether is purpose-built for a specific set of risks on the developer-machine AI-egress lane.

In scope — Tether addresses these
Inadvertent data exfil
Developer carelessness
Developers pasting proprietary code, credentials, or PII into AI completions or chat interfaces. Tier 0 floor + sync judge catch known patterns and fail-closed-on-timeout configurations.
Covered   For known patterns and configurations that route through the proxy.
Shadow AI tools
Unapproved coding agents
Developers installing extensions or CLIs that make AI API calls outside the approved toolchain. The proxy classifies the destination host on the CONNECT tunnel; unapproved vendors are blockable.
Covered   For tools that route through the proxy.
Policy tampering
Local config changes
A developer attempting to modify, downgrade, or replay an older policy. Ed25519 verification with monotonic version blocks both tampering and replay.
Covered
Compliance evidence gaps
Audit readiness
Inability to demonstrate technical controls. An append-only policy audit plus per-request attestation produce auditable evidence.
Covered
Out of scope — and we say so plainly

Tether is an endpoint product for the AI-egress lane, not a universal net. It doesn't govern server-side AI traffic from your pipelines, browser-only AI tools (that's your CASB's job today), agents that deliberately bypass the proxy, or a motivated insider with full machine access — it raises the cost there, it isn't a deterrent. We'd rather you read the honest edges than discover them. The full out-of-scope threat model, row by row, is in the technical brief →

Security disclosures

Coordinated disclosure policy.

If you find a vulnerability in Tether, we want to fix it quickly and credit you publicly. This policy describes scope, process, response timeline, and how to reach us privately.

Scope

In scope: code on master in arich2day/project-tether — Connect proxy (warden/cmd/proxy/), workspace daemon (warden/cmd/workspace/), Overwatch API (overwatch/api/), Overwatch console (overwatch/web/), VS Code extension (vscode-extension/), and the deploy Terraform (deploy/).

Out of scope: third-party dependencies (report upstream), Tether's marketing sites, social-engineering vectors, denial-of-service against a customer-deployed tenant (test against a tenant you own).

Process

Email a clear write-up, reproducer, and your preferred credit. We acknowledge within 48 hours, triage within 5 business days, and target a fix in 30 days for high-severity issues. We will coordinate disclosure timing with you.

Response time targets

Critical (RCE, signing-key compromise, auth bypass): 24-48h acknowledge · 7d fix target.
High (privilege escalation, policy verification bypass): 48h acknowledge · 30d fix target.
Medium / Low: 5 business days acknowledge · best-effort fix.

Compliance evidence

What we produce.
Not what we sign for you.

Tether is not itself SOC 2 or ISO 27001 certified — your auditor evaluates Tether as a control in your environment. What we produce is auditable evidence your auditor can map.

SOC 2 evidence

CC6 logical access (SCIM via Jackson + role-from-groups + audit). CC7 system monitoring (SIEM fan-out + posture heartbeats). CC8 change management (Ed25519-signed bundles + append-only audit + per-request attestation).

FedRAMP relevance

Per-request attestation an auditor re-verifies offline — decision, signed bundle, and public key in one artifact — is unusual at this stage, and it is the property federal-grade diligence looks for: per-request provenance that doesn't rest on trusting the vendor. We claim no FedRAMP status or authorization; we produce the evidence that kind of diligence asks for.

AI governance

Documented technical controls over AI data handling, audit trail of AI usage decisions, risk-based evaluation, policy versioning. Map this to your AI-governance program; we don't pretend to interpret the EU AI Act for you.
